# Acid House Smiley (1988)

> Warehouse darkness, acid yellow smileys, photocopied rave flyers — the visual language of Britain's Second Summer of Love.

## Origin

In the summer of 1987, four London DJs — Danny Rampling, Paul Oakenfold, Nicky Holloway, and Johnny Walker — took a trip to Ibiza and encountered the fusion of house music and outdoor hedonism that Alfredo Fioritto was pioneering at Amnesia. They brought it home. By 1988, Rampling's Shoom in a Southwark gym, Oakenfold's Future and Spectrum at Heaven, and Holloway's The Trip were ground zero for what the tabloids would call the Second Summer of Love. Chicago acid house — Phuture's *Acid Tracks*, the Roland TB-303's squelching bassline — collided with MDMA, and a disused warehouse network along the M25 motorway corridor became Britain's biggest illegal party circuit.

The smiley face was the movement's logo. Originally designed by Harvey Ball in 1963 for a Worcester insurance company, it had been repurposed by the Watchmen comic (1986) and then by designer Mark Pakenham for the Bomb the Bass *Beat Dis* sleeve in 1988. Printed on acid yellow backgrounds, photocopied onto A5 flyers handed out at service stations and phone boxes, the smiley became the universal signal: *rave tonight*. The Criminal Justice Act of 1994 — which specifically targeted "repetitive beats" — effectively criminalized the movement, but by then the smiley had entered permanent visual culture.

## Overview

Composition cues:
- **Layout**: Stacked vertical sections with bold horizontal dividers — mimicking the top-to-bottom read of a club flyer (date, DJs, location, directions).
- **Content width**: Container (1024–1280px) with acid yellow full-bleed accent blocks that break containment.
- **Framing**: Solid — heavy black backgrounds with hard-edge yellow and neon panels, no transparency or glass effects.
- **Grid intensity**: Strong — dense information layout echoing the overloaded warehouse flyer format.

## Colors

The palette is a neon arsenal on pure black. Acid yellow `#FFEC00` is the smiley itself — overwhelming, high-saturation, impossible to ignore, exactly the point. Hot pink `#FF2D87` comes from the rave flyer tradition of dayglo Risograph inks. Neon green `#39FF14` is glow-stick green, the color you see when the strobes cut out. Cyan `#00E5FF` provides info-tier accents. The black background `#0A0A0A` is the warehouse at 3am — no ambient light, just the strobes.

**Role usage**:
- Page background → `colors.background.page` (#0A0A0A — warehouse black)
- Card / surface background → `colors.background.surface` (#1A1A1A)
- Primary text and headlines → `colors.text.primary` (#FFEC00 — acid yellow)
- Primary action buttons → `colors.primary.500` (#FFEC00 with black text)
- Accent links and highlights → `colors.secondary.500` (#FF2D87 — hot pink)
- Success / live indicators → `colors.accent.500` (#39FF14 — neon green)
- Glow effects and focus rings → neon green or yellow rgba overlays
- Text on yellow backgrounds → `colors.text.inverse` (#0A0A0A)

## Elevation & Depth

There are no drop shadows in acid house — there is only glow. Every elevation cue is a neon light source radiating outward from the element, as if it were a UV-reactive surface under blacklight. The shadow scale is replaced entirely by glow intensity: `xs` is a barely perceptible yellow haze, `md` is the full smiley aura at `0 0 24px rgba(255, 236, 0, 0.6)`, and `2xl` stacks yellow and green glow for the hero section's supernova effect.

- Surface style: flat — no layering, no frosted glass.
- Blur: none — clarity is mandatory, this isn't vaporwave.
- Shadow ladder: all values are outward glows in acid yellow or neon green rgba, never downward offset box-shadows.
- Focus states use neon green glow rings (`0 0 0 3px rgba(57, 255, 20, 0.6)`).

## Shapes

- Buttons: `9999px` — pill-shaped, always. The visual pun is intentional.
- Cards: `16px` — rounded but not circular, like a flyer with clipped corners.
- Inputs: `9999px` — matching pill buttons for cohesion.
- Small badges and tags: `9999px`.
- No sharp corners anywhere — the aesthetic rounds everything.

## Motion

Motion is playful and energetic — things pop in, glow pulses, and hover states have bounce. This is 1988, the strobes are going, the 303 is squelching, nothing is subtle. But motion serves the UI, not the rave — interactions should feel responsive and alive, not nauseating.

- Level: playful.
- Durations: fast 120ms for micro-interactions, normal 250ms for transitions, slow 400ms for glow pulses.
- Easings: spring `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` for enter animations — that overshoot bounce.
- Hover patterns: glow (yellow aura intensifies), scale (subtle 1.02 lift), tint (background color shift).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: respected — disable glow pulses and scale, keep instant color transitions.

## Do's & Don'ts

### ✓ Do
- Use acid yellow `#FFEC00` for all primary actions and headlines — it's the smiley, it's the brand.
- Make buttons pill-shaped (`border-radius: 9999px`) — lean into the visual joke.
- Use glow effects (`box-shadow` with neon rgba) instead of traditional drop shadows.
- Set all headlines in uppercase Anton — channel the warehouse flyer energy.
- Use Permanent Marker for handwritten overlay callouts — the secret-location scrawl.

### ✗ Don't
- Use tasteful muted palettes — this is dayglo, not Danish.
- Switch to light mode — the warehouse is dark, the design stays dark.
- Use serif body text — this is warehouse concrete, not library oak.
- Put anything on cream backgrounds — the only light surface is acid yellow.
- Apply subtle gradients — colors are flat blocks, hard-edge, Risograph logic.
- Aim for polished corporate design — this should feel photocopied, not Figma-polished.
- Use drop shadows that imply depth — elevation is glow, not gravity.
- Fall into modern minimalism — the flyer was overloaded and that was the point.

## Applications

This system is built for music, events, and nightlife — lineup announcements, festival landing pages, DJ portfolio sites, club night promotions. It also works for any brand that wants to signal subcultural credibility, youthful energy, and deliberate anti-corporate attitude: streetwear drops, zine publishing platforms, underground art collectives. The high-contrast yellow-on-black is inherently accessible for low-light viewing (phone screens in dark venues), and the bold type hierarchy ensures scanability even at a glance.
