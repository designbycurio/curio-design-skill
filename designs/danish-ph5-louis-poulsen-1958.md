# Danish PH5 Louis Poulsen (1958)

> Engineered light — every curve calculated, not a single millimeter decoration.

## Origin

The PH5 pendant lamp was released in October 1958 by Louis Poulsen, Copenhagen. Its designer, Poul Henningsen — architect, critic, and Denmark's most consequential lighting designer — had been working with the company since 1925, when director Sophus Kaastrup-Olsen commissioned his first three-shade PH lamp. By 1958, Henningsen faced a new problem: mass-market low-wattage incandescent bulbs could be installed by anyone, making glare control critical. His solution was a five-layered shade system (hence "PH5") that distributes light evenly regardless of bulb type or hanging height. The lamp became ubiquitous — hung in nearly every Danish apartment between 1960 and 1985.

Louis Poulsen's brand identity evolved around this engineering precision. Product photography uses a signature blue-gray backdrop. Technical cross-section diagrams explain the light physics. Typography is restrained sans-serif at light weights with tight tracking. Hairline rules separate content. Generous negative space dominates every layout. The visual language shares DNA with parallel Danish-modern designers — Arne Jacobsen's furniture, Verner Panton's later experiments for Louis Poulsen — but remains distinct in its insistence that aesthetics are a byproduct of engineering, never the goal.

The palette is drawn from Louis Poulsen's product photography and archive materials. The dominant backdrop is Poulsen blue-gray `#7F8C99` — the specific cool midtone used behind studio-lit product shots, never cream or beige. Surfaces are PH white `#E8E4D8`, a warm-but-not-cream Danish off-white with a faint green undertone characteristic of mid-century Scandinavian interiors. ## Elevation & Depth

Depth is communicated through color-plane separation rather than shadows. The blue-gray page `#7F8C99` sits behind warm off-white surfaces `#E8E4D8`, creating a recessive-versus-forward layering that references the way a PH lamp hangs in mid-air against a Danish apartment wall. Shadows are barely-there whispers — `0 1px 2px rgba(0,0,0,0.06)` at most — because Henningsen's design philosophy treats light as the subject, not shadow. Hairline borders in cool gray provide edge definition when surfaces meet.

- Surface style: solid — opaque PH-white panels on blue-gray ground
- Blur: none — no glass, no translucency
- Shadow ladder: whisper-light (max 0.06 opacity), never prominent
- Hairline borders: 1px solid `#b0b5bc` for structural separation
- Depth cue: background-color contrast between page and surface

Hover states shift with quiet opacity changes or subtle brass-tint washes. Nothing bounces, springs, or overshoots. ## Do's & Don'ts

### ✓ Do
- Use Poulsen blue-gray (#7F8C99) as the primary page backdrop
- Set headings at light weight (300) with tight tracking for whisper-authority
- Use brass (#C19A4A) for primary actions and accent lines
- Leave generous negative space — emptiness communicates precision

### ✗ Don't
- Use cream or wallpaper-warm backgrounds (the backdrop is blue-gray, not beige)
- Introduce saturated palettes or chromatic brand colors
- Add drop shadows or elevation effects
- Apply decorative ornament or flourish
- Set headings in bold serif display faces
- Clutter layouts — every element must earn its space
- Use pastel washes or soft gradients
- Apply modern startup-style gradient backgrounds
