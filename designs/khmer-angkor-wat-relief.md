# Khmer Angkor Wat Relief

> Warm rust-ochre sandstone, continuous Apsara frieze, lotus-petal bands — the longest narrative bas-relief in the world, distilled to a strict monochrome discipline.

## Origin

Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument on Earth, built between 1113 and 1150 under the Khmer king Suryavarman II as a Hindu temple dedicated to Vishnu, later converted to Buddhist use. Its third gallery houses the longest continuous narrative bas-relief in the world — 600 meters of shallow-relief carving in warm sandstone depicting the Churning of the Sea of Milk, the Battle of Kurukshetra from the Mahabharata, the Battle of Lanka from the Ramayana, scenes of Heaven and Hell, and Suryavarman II's own military procession.

After the Khmer Empire's collapse in 1431, Angkor was reclaimed by jungle for four centuries until French naturalist Henri Mouhot reintroduced it to Europe in 1860. Two decades of EFEO archaeology, the foundational scholarship of George Cœdès, Auguste Rodin's obsessive Apsara sketches, post-Khmer-Rouge cultural reconstruction under APSARA Authority, and UNESCO World Heritage designation in 1992 have all kept this 12th-century carved-stone aesthetic alive as Cambodia's defining visual identity.

## Overview

Composition cues:
- **Layout**: Horizontal frieze procession — wide content blocks running left-to-right like the continuous gallery wall, with figure-overlap density rather than negative space.
- **Content width**: Wide (1024–1280px). Avoid narrow editorial column — Angkor needs the breadth of the gallery wall.

- **Grid intensity**: Strong 8px rhythm. Mandala-aligned symmetry where possible — concentric, axial, never asymmetric for asymmetry's sake.

## Colors

Strict warm-sandstone monotone. The discipline is light-and-shadow modulation on a single ochre family — never saturated, never cool. Cream-highlight `#D8B898` is the page ground (raised relief surface in dawn light); deep umber `#3D2A1A` is the shadow recess in the deepest carving cut. Sunset-gold `#E0A23A` is the only true chromatic accent, reserved for hover states and the rare focal mark. Jungle moss `#5E6F4A` appears only as semantic success.

**Role usage**:
- Page background → `colors.background.page` (#D8B898 sandstone cream-highlight)
- Card / content surface → `colors.background.surface` (#E8D2B8 lighter content card)
- Hero block / processional frieze → `colors.background.subtle` (#CEA572 deeper sandstone)
- Body text → `colors.text.primary` (#3D2A1A deep umber-shadow, never absolute black)
- Muted / caption → `colors.text.muted` (#7F5A3D weathered-darker)

## Typography

Monumental and lapidary. Cinzel and Trajan Pro give the same Roman-capital gravity that Khmer stonecutters achieved in Old Khmer and Sanskrit inscriptions — generous letter-spacing, no flourishes, weight that reads at architectural scale. EB Garamond and Cormorant Garamond carry body text with humanist warmth. Noto Khmer and Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit support the native scripts; Khmer "អង្គរវត្ត" and Sanskrit phrases are treated as graphic motifs at hero scale.

## Elevation & Depth

Bas-relief shadow emboss. Surfaces sit on the page like shallow stone carvings — every card carries a soft directional shadow from above, and an inner highlight along the top edge suggesting raised relief catching dawn light. No glassmorphism, no blur — Angkor is **solid carved stone**.

- Surface style: layered (cream-highlight surface raised above sandstone ground)
- Blur: none
- Shadow ladder: xs hairline → 2xl deep-recess. All shadows use the deep-umber `rgba(61, 42, 26, ...)` tint rather than neutral black, to stay inside the warm-sandstone family.

## Shapes

- Corner radii: none 0 / sm 2 / md 4 / lg 8 / xl 12 / full 9999. Predominantly square or 4–8px softened. Khmer architecture is rectilinear stone; never aggressively rounded.
- Decorative shapes appear via multi-foil arch silhouettes (gopura motif) and lotus-petal-band dividers, not via border-radius.

## Motion

Restrained and ritual. Movements feel like sandstone catching the rising sun — slow tonal shifts rather than springy bounce. 320ms is the default; hovers lift 2px with a deepening shadow, links underline with an ochre stroke that draws right-to-left like a pen-stroke on rubbing paper.

- Level: restrained
- Durations: instant 0 / fast 160 / normal 320 / slow 520 / slower 800 ms
- Easings: gentle cubic-bezier defaults; spring (1.56 overshoot) reserved for the rare focal reveal
- Hover patterns: lift (translateY -2px) + tint (deepen sandstone) + stroke (ochre underline)
- reducedMotion: respected — cross-fade only

## Do's & Don'ts

### ✓ Do
- Use Angkor sandstone `#B07C4F` for primary CTAs — the canonical 12th-century stone color.
- Treat every section divider as a lotus-petal-band ornament strip, not a plain hairline.
- Apply the bas-relief shadow emboss (top cream highlight + bottom umber recess) to cards and buttons to suggest carved stone depth.
- Use specific Khmer vocabulary in motifs — Apsara, kāla, naga, gopura, lotus-bud — and label them by name.
- Pair Cinzel monumental capitals with EB Garamond body, both with generous letter-spacing in the heading scale.

### ✗ Don't
- Saturated bright color — Angkor is **warm-sandstone monochrome**.
- Pure black text — use deep umber-shadow `#3D2A1A` instead.
- Modern sans-serif (Inter / Geist forbidden) — type must support Khmer + Sanskrit and carry lapidary weight.
- Photographic full-color illustration — imagery must feel **monochrome bas-relief**.
- Sleek minimal negative-space — Angkor is **continuous-frieze density**.
- Generic "Asian temple" stereotype — use specific Khmer vocabulary.
- Modern flat icons — use stone-carved-relief 3D shadow treatment.
- Cool blue-grey palette — warm sandstone-ochre is the discipline.

## Applications

Best for cultural-heritage publications, museum digital experiences, archaeology and Southeast Asian studies sites, luxury travel brands evoking Cambodia and the Mekong region, scholarly editions of Hindu-Buddhist epics, and high-end editorial features where monumental gravity matters more than airy modernism. Particularly strong for long-form narrative content — the continuous-frieze layout was literally designed for storytelling at architectural scale. Avoid for fast SaaS dashboards, fintech, or any context demanding cool restraint.
