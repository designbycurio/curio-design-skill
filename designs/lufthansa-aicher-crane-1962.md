# Lufthansa (Aicher)

> A navy crane-in-disc on bold melon-yellow, governed by a strict lowercase Helvetica-Bold grid.

## Origin
In 1962 Lufthansa commissioned the Ulm School of Design (HfG Ulm) to rebuild its identity, and Otl Aicher, with the student team known as Gruppe E5, answered with 'Projekt 1400' — a complete system rather than a single mark. The Kranich (crane), a Lufthansa emblem since the 1920s, was redrawn as pure geometry locked inside a defined circle, ready to sit on tailfins, tickets, signage, and stationery at any scale.

What made the work historic was its rigor. Aicher delivered a constants manual: a modular grid, a single typographic voice in lowercase Helvetica-Bold, fixed color values, and clear placement rules. Saturated melon-yellow fields carried navy and black marks with no texture, gradient, or ornament. The result became the textbook example of systematic corporate identity and shaped Aicher's later masterwork — the pictograms for the 1972 Munich Olympics.

## Overview
Composition cues:
- **Layout**: Strict modular grid; everything snaps to columns and an 8px rhythm.
- **Content width**: Container-bound, disciplined measure; no full-bleed sprawl.
- **Framing**: Solid panels and hard 2px rules — white surfaces sit cleanly on the yellow ground.
- **Grid intensity**: Strong — the grid is visibly the governing logic, not a hidden aid.

## Colors
The palette is a closed, functional set: a bold saturated melon-yellow ground carrying a deep navy crane, with black and a darker Maastricht blue as supporting marks and white for relief panels. Color is structural, never atmospheric — large flat fields, full saturation, hard edges.
**Role usage**:
- Page background → `colors.background.page` (`#FFAD00`)
- Crane / primary marks & headings → `colors.primary.500` (`#05164D`)
- Card and content surfaces → `colors.background.surface` (`#FFFFFF`)
- Secondary marks / deep accents → `colors.secondary.500` (`#0A1D3D`)
- Body text on yellow or white → `colors.text.primary` (`#05164D`)
- Hard structural rules & icons → `#000000`
- Inverse text on navy fills → `colors.text.inverse` (`#FFFFFF`)

## Typography
One voice does all the work: lowercase Helvetica-Bold, set tight and confident, with size and weight — not family — carrying hierarchy. Headlines stay lowercase, body stays even-toned, and tracking is neutral to slightly tight. No serifs, no decorative cuts.
**Text styles**:
- `display-xl` — Helvetica Neue, 96px, weight 700, line-height 1.0, letter-spacing -0.04em
- `display-lg` — Helvetica Neue, 64px, weight 700, line-height 1.05, letter-spacing -0.03em
- `heading-1` — Helvetica Neue, 36px, weight 700, line-height 1.1, letter-spacing -0.02em
- `body-lg` — Helvetica Neue, 18px, weight 400, line-height 1.5, letter-spacing 0
- `body-md` — Helvetica Neue, 16px, weight 400, line-height 1.5, letter-spacing 0
- `caption` — Helvetica Neue, 12px, weight 500, line-height 1.375, letter-spacing 0.05em
- `mono-md` — Helvetica Neue, 16px, weight 500, line-height 1.5, letter-spacing 0

## Elevation & Depth
The surface is flat by doctrine — depth comes from contrast and the grid, not from material. Shadows are nearly absent; where a panel must lift on interaction, a faint navy-tinted shadow is the only concession.
- Surface style: flat color fields, hard edges, no blur.
- Blur: none.
- Shadow ladder: xs/sm for subtle separation, md/lg reserved for card hover, never ambient.

## Shapes
- Corner radii: effectively square — 0 to 2px on controls, 4–8px only at the largest scale.
- The defining curve is the crane's containing circle (`radius.full`), used deliberately as a mark, not as UI softening.

## Motion
Motion is restrained and mechanical — quick, linear-feeling transitions that respect the system's precision. Nothing bounces or floats; state changes read as crisp switches.
- Level: restrained.
- Durations: fast 120ms for hovers, normal 250ms for transitions.
- Easings: standard ease for most, ease-out for entrances.
- Hover patterns: tint (navy ↔ deeper navy), stroke (border fill-in), underline for links.

## Techniques

### Crane-in-disc mark
A navy crane figure precisely centered in a defined circle — the system's anchor mark.
```css
.crane-disc {
  width: 96px;
  height: 96px;
  border-radius: 9999px;
  background: #05164D;
  display: grid;
  place-items: center;
}
.crane-disc svg { width: 56px; height: 56px; fill: #FFAD00; }
```

### Lowercase Helvetica-Bold lockup
Tight, all-lowercase wordmark lockups that sit on the grid baseline.
```css
.lockup {
  font-family: 'Helvetica Neue', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif;
  font-weight: 700;
  text-transform: lowercase;
  letter-spacing: -0.03em;
  line-height: 1.0;
  color: #05164D;
}
```

### Flat yellow field block
Large edge-to-edge melon-yellow panels divided by hard navy rules — the systematic surface.
```css
.field-block {
  background: #FFAD00;
  color: #05164D;
  padding: 96px 48px;
  border-bottom: 2px solid #05164D;
}
```

## Iconography
Icons are custom, geometric, and filled — built from the same circle-and-line vocabulary as the crane mark. They read as solid silhouettes rather than line drawings, with no stroke decoration. Keep them flat navy or black on yellow, sized on the grid (16/20/24px).
- Treatment: filled, geometric.
- Set: custom (crane-family geometry).
- Stroke: 0px — shapes are solid, not outlined.

## Do's & Don'ts
### ✓ Do
- Use the saturated melon-yellow `#FFAD00` as the actual page ground, not a tint.
- Set all headings in lowercase Helvetica-Bold and let size carry hierarchy.
- Keep the crane locked inside its circle and align every element to the grid.
- Render surfaces flat with hard 2px navy rules — contrast over shadow.
- Reserve navy `#05164D` for primary marks, headings, and key actions.

### ✗ Don't
- Never use cream/ivory or plain-white page backgrounds — the ground is the bold Aicher yellow.
- No pastel or desaturated yellow — hold RAL 1028 saturation at `#FFAD00`.
- No serif or decorative type — strict lowercase Helvetica-Bold only.
- No ornament, gradient, or texture — preserve flat, grid-driven surfaces.

## Applications
Best fit for corporate identity systems, transport and aviation branding, signage and wayfinding, and any product that wants to signal Swiss/Ulm-school rigor. It excels for design-system documentation, B2B dashboards, and editorial layouts where a single confident typographic voice and a bold flat ground do all the talking.
