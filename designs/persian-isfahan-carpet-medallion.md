# Persian Isfahan Carpet

> A single central medallion + radiating quartered corners + 800-knot-per-inch palmette-arabesque density — the floor as a calligraphic garden.

## Origin

Persian Isfahan medallion carpets are the peak of Safavid-era weaving (1501–1736), produced under Shah Abbas I's royal workshops in Isfahan after he relocated the court there in 1598. Tabriz, Kashan, Kerman, and Qom shared the canonical vocabulary: a central diamond-medallion with sixteen radiating pendants, quartered corner spandrels, and a sequence of one main border framed by two to four minor bands. The Ardabil Carpet at the V&A (1539–40, signed by Maqsud Kashani) is the masterwork.

The aesthetic spread across Mughal India, Ottoman Turkey, and Andalusian Spain, then ignited a 19th-century European salon boom that founded modern carpet-scholarship. Scholars like Jon Thompson, Murray Eiland, and Sheila Canby have continued documenting the tradition, while contemporary master-weavers in Isfahan and Kashan still tie 200–800 knots per inch using madder, indigo, weld-yellow, walnut-husk, and cochineal. UNESCO has designated multiple Iranian carpet techniques as Intangible Heritage.

## Do's & Don'ts

### ✓ Do
- Anchor every hero with a central medallion plus four quartered corner spandrels — symmetric on both axes
- Fill empty surfaces with low-density palmette-arabesque infill; horror-vacui is the discipline, not a flaw
- Use saturated natural-dye jewel-tones — midnight-blue, crimson, saffron, jade, ivory — and let faint abrash variation breathe
- Frame content with multi-band borders: one main band plus two to four minor bands, each with distinct pattern
- Set body type in Garamond-family serifs and respect right-to-left reading for any Arabic/Persian phrase

### ✗ Don't
- Use modern sans-serif fonts like Inter or Geist — pick faces that support Arabic and Persian RTL scripts
- Drop the palette into pastels or faded tints — every hue must feel like wool dyed in copper vats
- Default to sleek minimalism with empty white space — Isfahan is horror-vacui arabesque density
- Compose asymmetric or off-balance layouts — strict bilateral symmetry around both axes is mandatory
- Use photographic illustration that breaks the wool-knot pile-rug surface fiction
- Ship on a pure white page — always start from the cream-ivory carpet-margin tone
- Reach for generic geometric-tile clipart — use the specific Isfahan vocabulary (medallion, corner-quartered, cloud-band, palmette-arabesque)
- Boost colors into synthetic neon brightness — natural-dye warmth with subtle abrash is the rule
