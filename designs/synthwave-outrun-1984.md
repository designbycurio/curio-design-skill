# Synthwave Outrun 1984

> Neon-drenched retro-futurism — chrome type, infinite grids, and Miami sunset gradients for an 80s that never was.

## Origin

Synthwave — also called outrun or retrowave — is an internet-native aesthetic that crystallized between 2010 and 2014, drawing from the visual language of Sega's Out Run arcade cabinet (1986), Miami Vice's pastel-and-neon title sequence (1984), Tron's glowing digital frontier (1982), and the neon-noir of Blade Runner (1982). Musicians like Kavinsky (whose "Nightcall" soundtracked Nicolas Winding Refn's Drive in 2011), FM-84, and The Midnight provided the sonic backbone, while digital artists Signalnoise (James White) and Robert Beatty defined its look: chrome typography, vanishing-point grid floors, and sunset gradients bleeding from deep purple through hot pink to burning orange.

Unlike vaporwave's ironic, pastel-tinted critique of consumer culture, synthwave is earnestly nostalgic — it loves the 1980s without reservation. The aesthetic peaked between 2014 and 2018, gained mainstream visibility through Stranger Things' title sequence (2016), and continues as a living internet subculture anchored in communities like /r/outrun. Every element is designed to glow against the dark.

## Overview

Composition cues:
- **Layout**: Grid-based with strong vanishing-point perspective elements
- **Content width**: Wide — dramatic compositions need horizontal room
- **Framing**: Bordered with neon-glow edges; cards and panels outlined in cyan light
- **Grid intensity**: Strong — the perspective grid floor is the defining compositional motif

## Colors

The synthwave palette is a Miami sunset collapsed into pure light. Deep purple (#4a0e6e) anchors the sky, bleeding through hot magenta-pink (#c8197f) into burning orange (#ff7a3c) at the horizon. Against this gradient, neon cyan (#00f0e8) traces grid lines and structural accents, while hot pink (#ff1493) pulses as the primary action color. The ground is near-black — the aesthetic is night-coded by definition, with all color existing as emitted light against darkness.

**Role usage**:
- Page background → `colors.background.page` (#0a0014, near-black with violet undertone)
- Card / panel surface → `colors.background.surface` (#1a0030, deep purple-black)
- Primary action (buttons, links) → `colors.primary.500` (#ff1493, hot pink neon)
- Structural accents (borders, grid lines, outlines) → `colors.secondary.500` (#00f0e8, cyan neon)
- Warm accent (badges, highlights, sunset echoes) → `colors.accent.500` (#ff7a3c, sunset orange)
- Body text → `colors.text.primary` (#f0ecf5, cool lavender white)
- Muted / secondary text → `colors.text.secondary` (#b8a4d1, soft purple)

## Typography

Type in synthwave speaks in two registers: chrome display and digital readout. Headings use Audiowide — a wide, single-weight display face that evokes arcade cabinet marquees and VHS cover art — for maximum neon-sign impact. Body text uses Orbitron, a geometric sans-serif with a machine-precise, digital-dashboard feel. Code and data use JetBrains Mono for a hacker-terminal aesthetic. All type is set with generous letter-spacing to let each character breathe in neon light, and uppercase transforms on buttons and labels amplify the arcade-sign energy.

**Text styles**:
- `display-xl` — Audiowide, 96px, weight 400, line-height 1.0, letter-spacing -0.02em
- `display-lg` — Audiowide, 64px, weight 400, line-height 1.1, letter-spacing -0.02em
- `heading-1` — Audiowide, 48px, weight 400, line-height 1.2, letter-spacing 0
- `body-lg` — Orbitron, 18px, weight 400, line-height 1.625, letter-spacing 0.05em
- `body-md` — Orbitron, 16px, weight 400, line-height 1.5, letter-spacing 0.05em
- `caption` — Orbitron, 12px, weight 500, line-height 1.5, letter-spacing 0.05em
- `mono-md` — JetBrains Mono, 14px, weight 400, line-height 1.625, letter-spacing 0

## Elevation & Depth

Synthwave depth is created entirely through light emission, not shadow. In the real world, synthwave elements are neon tubes, CRT screens, and laser beams — they cast light outward, not shadows downward. Surfaces are dark and recessive; the glow itself creates the hierarchy. Cards sit behind a faint cyan aura, active buttons pulse with pink radiance, and the background grid recedes through atmospheric perspective into deep purple.

- Surface style: Layered — dark translucent panels over gradient/grid backgrounds
- Blur: 12px backdrop-blur on glassmorphic overlays for depth separation
- Shadow ladder: Neon glow replaces traditional box-shadow — `0 0 8px`, `0 0 12px`, `0 0 20px`, `0 0 40px` with neon color at decreasing opacity
- Focus rings: Hot pink glow (`0 0 0 3px rgba(255,20,147,0.5)`)

## Shapes

- Corner radii are minimal — synthwave is angular, not rounded
- Buttons and inputs: 2px radius (barely perceptible, machine-precise)
- Cards: 4px radius
- Badges and pills: 9999px (full-round for glowing pill shapes)
- Modals and panels: 8px radius

## Motion

Motion in synthwave is lively and theatrical — elements materialize with neon flicker, hover states intensify glow radiance, and transitions carry the kinetic energy of an 80s sports car. Nothing is subtle; the aesthetic demands presence. Animations should feel like turning on a neon sign — a brief surge of light that settles into a steady glow.

- Level: lively
- Durations: fast 120ms for micro-interactions; normal 250ms for state changes; slow 400ms for entrance animations; slower 600ms for dramatic reveals
- Easings: default `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` for most transitions; spring `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1.5, 0.36, 1)` for playful overshoots on hover
- Hover patterns: glow intensification (primary), lift + scale for cards, tint shift for text links

## Iconography

Icons are linear and lightweight — thin strokes of cyan or pink light against dark surfaces, like wire-frame renderings on an 80s CRT terminal. They should feel technical and precise, never filled or heavy, reinforcing the aesthetic of light-as-structure.

- Treatment: linear (outline only, glowing on hover)
- Set: Lucide — clean geometric linework pairs well with Orbitron body text
- Stroke: 1.5px default; icons gain a subtle cyan text-shadow glow on interactive states

## Do's & Don'ts

### ✓ Do
- Use hot pink (#ff1493) for all primary interactive elements — buttons, links, active states
- Apply neon glow box-shadows on accents, borders, and focus states — glow is mandatory
- Compose hero sections as sunset gradients bleeding from purple through pink to orange
- Set headings in Audiowide uppercase with generous letter-spacing for arcade-marquee energy
- Embrace the drama — this aesthetic rewards boldness, not restraint

### ✗ Don't
- Use pastels — soft pink, baby blue, or lavender belong to vaporwave, not synthwave
- Apply earthy or natural color palettes — no greens, browns, or warm neutrals
- Use light backgrounds — synthwave is night-coded; white or cream kills the glow
- Default to restrained typographic hierarchy — every heading should command attention
- Fall back to modern flat design — if elements don't glow, they don't belong

## Applications

Synthwave Outrun 1984 is ideal for music platforms, gaming dashboards, event landing pages, portfolio sites for creative professionals, and any product that wants to project bold, nostalgic energy. It works exceptionally well for single-page marketing sites, launch countdown pages, and media-heavy experiences where dramatic visual impact matters more than information density. The dark foundation and high-contrast neon palette also make it surprisingly readable for long-form content when body text is set in cool lavender-white against deep purple-black surfaces.
